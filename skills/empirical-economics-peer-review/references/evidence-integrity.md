# Reviewer evidence integrity

Use these rules when deciding whether a concern, requested revision, or recommendation is supported by the manuscript.

- Do not invent results, coefficients, sample sizes, robustness checks, sources, or institutional facts.
- Distinguish missing evidence from negative evidence and from evidence that contradicts the claim.
- Check table-text consistency for signs, magnitudes, significance, observation counts, sample periods, variable names, fixed effects, and clustering.
- Preserve source and provenance information for data, references, and formal outputs.
- Label exploratory versus confirmatory analyses and do not treat post-hoc explanations as pre-specified theory.
- Require mechanism evidence to match the claimed channel; a correlated mediator is not automatically a validated mechanism.
- Distinguish a subsample result from a formal interaction test of subgroup differences.
- Treat frozen Stata output as the formal empirical source when the project has defined Stata/reghdfe as its authority. Report conflicts instead of silently correcting values.
- A recommendation should state which evidence would materially change it and whether the requested remedy changes identification or only presentation/precision.
